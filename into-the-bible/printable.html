What is the Bible All About?

How We Got the Bible & Why We Can Trust It — speaker notes

SESSION #810  ·  YOUTH CURRICULUM 2019  ·  AUDIENCE 14–15  ·  ~1h 35 MIN

Running the deck
  • Open index.html in a browser and press F (or F11) for full screen — there's no on-screen prompt, just the key. A 16:9 projector fills edge to edge; other ratios get black bars, never cropped content.
  • Click the left 10% of the screen to go back, the right 10% to go forward.
  • Click the middle to reveal the next bullet — nothing else. Most slides build a list one line at a time. The five prayer slides (2–4 and 23–24) have no reveals: the full text is on screen from the start, so a click in the middle does nothing there — use the right edge or the arrow key to move on.
  • Keys: → / space forward (reveal, then advance) · ← back (un-reveal, then go back) · Home / End jump to the ends. The mouse wheel and a trackpad swipe also move through the deck, so don't scroll by accident mid-session.
  • The dots bottom-left are one dot per reveal on the current slide; the lit ones are already showing. Bottom-right is the slide number.
  • Videos: each video has a slide to itself (6 and 11) — a title and a poster, nothing to read. Click the poster to play, then click the ✕ in the top-right of the video to close it. Playback also stops automatically the moment you move to another slide, so audio can never carry over.
  • The URL keeps your place — #slide-9&shown-3 means slide 9 with 3 lines revealed. Reload or bookmark it to resume.
Before class — nothing needs setting up.
Videos. Slide 6 is Fr. Mike Schmitz's The Bible in a Nutshell and slide 11 is BibleProject's What is the Bible?. Both YouTube IDs are hard-coded in the VIDEOS object at the top of the script, so the poster plays on the first click — the paste-a-link box never appears. If you ever need to swap one out, blank that slot's ID in VIDEOS and the box will offer itself on the next click; whatever you paste is saved to localStorage and survives a reload.

Images are in place. img/good-samaritan.jpg (slide 18), img/carlo-acutis.jpg and img/pier-giorgio-frassati.jpg (slide 19) are all real and load as shipped. Swap in better files under the same names and nothing else needs touching. The Good Samaritan is a public-domain painting by W. H. Margetson; if you replace it, keep a tall crop so it doesn't fight the text beside it.

Where this differs from the original outline. Both ends of the session are now framed by a prayer: an opening prayer on slides 2–4 before the check-in, and "Come, Holy Spirit" on slides 23–24 at the close. The running order also changed — the Fr. Mike Schmitz video and the "one story, six stages" recap now sit immediately after the hands-up check-in (6–7), so the session opens with the big picture rather than the setup, and the "why read the Bible?" framing follows on 8–9. Each video has its own dedicated slide (6 and 11) with the talking points moved to the slide after it (7 and 12). Inspiration is split across two slides (13 and 14) rather than cramming two verses, a Greek word and four catechism points onto one screen. The closing slides for "Let us pray", the Our Father and the dismissal have been replaced by the two-slide closing prayer; the dismissal lines and the closing song are preserved in the notes for slide 24. Everything else follows the outline.

5

Opening vote — hands up

2 min
On screen Five statements, revealed one at a time: opened a Bible in the last week / in the last month / have one at home I've actually read — not just used as a coaster / read a whole book on my own / wanted to but didn't know where to start
"No judgment, no wrong answers. Just raise your hand if it's true for you. We're just getting a vibe check."

Notes: Read each line, then count the hands out loud — the drop-off between #1 and #5 is the whole conversation. If almost nobody raises a hand at #1, that's your hook: "so let's figure out why that is."

One story, six stages

The big question

1 min
On screen But… how did the Bible actually get written? — Like, what was the process? What do you think?
"We know why we should read it. But have you ever stopped and wondered how we got it in the first place? Like, how did God actually get His words onto paper?"

Notes: Take two or three guesses from the room here. Write the good wrong ones on the board — you'll use them on slide 10.

Wrong answers only

5 min

Notes: Add the guesses the room gave you on slide 9 to this list as you reveal. The last one always gets a laugh — let it. Then land the pivot: "so what is the real answer?"

12

Key takeaways

5 min
On screen A small library, not one book · many authors over 1,000+ years · OT: Torah, Prophets, Writings · NT: Gospels, Letters, Acts · Catholic Bibles add 7 more books

Notes: This slide carries the single most important idea in the session, because it is the answer to slide 10. Don't rush it.

"God used real human authors — their personalities, their styles, their languages — but He inspired them so that what they wrote was exactly what He wanted. It's like when a teacher gives you a project outline, but you still write it in your own words. Except God's outline is perfect."
13

What does "inspired" actually mean?

6 min
On screen 2 Timothy 3:16 and 2 Peter 1:21 in full, then the Greek θεόπνευστος broken out as theos (God) + pneuo (to breathe) = "God-breathed"
"Two key verses. That's it. God breathed into the authors, and they wrote. It's like when you're gaming and you have a really good coach guiding you — you're still playing, but they're making sure you don't mess up."

Notes: The Greek word usually lands hard — it's the same word that gave us "pneumatic." Point at it: theos + pneuo, God + breath. Say it out loud, theop-NOO-stos. If a Greek kid is in the room, this is their moment.

14

What the Church teaches

3 min
On screen God is the author of Scripture (CCC 105) · He chose human authors to write what He wanted (CCC 106) · Inspiration = God-breathed, the Spirit carrying them as they wrote · The Bible teaches truth for our salvation (CCC 107) — then "God is the author. The authors are real. Both things are true at once."

Notes: This slide exists to slow the idea down. The wrong answers on slide 10 usually assume that "God wrote it" and "humans wrote it" are contradictory. Walk the four points slowly, then land the last line — that's the whole answer to slide 10 in one sentence. The teacher-and-project-outline comparison is the one to say out loud; use whichever slide the room is on when you get there (it's on 12 and 14).

15

Ancient figures vs. the Bible

4 min
On screen A four-column comparison table with the New Testament row highlighted, then "If you trust your history textbook about Plato, you should absolutely trust the Gospels."
Ancient work Written Earliest copies Copies known
Plato's works ~400 BC 1,200 years later 7
Virgil's Aeneid ~20 BC 350 years later A few
New Testament ~50–100 AD 30–50 years later 5,000+ (Greek)
"Real talk: if you trust what your history textbook says about Plato, you should absolutely trust the Gospels. Seven copies, and the earliest is over a thousand years after he died. The New Testament? Thousands of Greek manuscripts, and some copied within a few decades. It's not even close."

Notes: If a student pushes back with "but Plato is a philosopher, this is a religious claim" — that's a good sign, take it. The point isn't that Plato and the Gospels are the same kind of claim; it's that if textual evidence is your standard for Plato, the Gospels clear it by an absurd margin.

16

The "so what?" — the trilemma

3 min
On screen Three options: A — Liar · B — Lunatic · C — Lord (highlighted), then "His teachings and His impact rule out the other two."
"Some people say, 'Yeah, but believing in Plato is low stakes. If Jesus was wrong, that's a big deal.' Exactly. So let's look at what Jesus actually taught. If He was crazy, His teachings would be… crazy. Right?"

Notes: All three cards look the same except the gold one — nothing is greyed out or crossed off, so nothing is visually "punished." Note that Lord is gold from the moment the slide appears; the reveal is the verdict line, then a second line: "Nobody calls a liar or a madman the most influential teacher in human history — and then writes the parables that keep happening anyway." Read the two wrong options out loud in the same tone as the right one, click once to land the verdict, pause, click again. This is often called C. S. Lewis's trilemma; name-dropping it is optional.

17

Teachings that changed the world

3 min
On screen "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you." (Matthew 5:44) · "The last will be first, and the first will be last." (Matthew 20:16) — beside a two-figure drawing labelled CHASING LIKES and BEING FIRST
"Think about your school. Everyone's trying to be popular. Everyone's trying to level up their social status. Then Jesus comes in and says, 'Actually, the popular kids will be last, and the quiet kid in the corner will be first.' That's not just counter-cultural — that's insane. But it's also… true."
18

More teachings that shocked the world

3 min
On screen A painting of the Good Samaritan lifting the wounded man, beside The Good Samaritan — a rival-group outsider becomes the hero (Luke 10:25–37) · Forgive seventy-seven times, no limits (Matthew 18:21–22) with a 77× dial · "Peter asked if seven times was enough. Jesus said: nah — seventy-seven."
"The Good Samaritan? To the people listening, a Samaritan was like… imagine if Jesus made the hero of the story someone from a rival school, or someone everyone makes fun of. That would be like making the class clown the valedictorian. It was shocking. And forgiving 77 times? The rabbis said 3 times was enough. Jesus said, 'Nah, keep going.' That's not 'duh' advice — that's revolutionary."
19

Lives changed by the Gospel

4 min
On screen Saint Carlo Acutis (1991–2006) · Saint Pier Giorgio Frassati (1901–1925) · "They were normal teens. The Gospel made them extraordinary."
"These aren't ancient people in robes. Carlo was a gamer. Pier Giorgio loved hiking and pranking his friends. They were like you. But they took Jesus seriously, and their lives became proof that the Gospel is real."

Notes: Both portraits load from img/ and are already in place. This is the slide teens remember — don't rush it. Carlo died at 15, which is worth saying out loud plainly.

20

The New Testament completes the Old

3 min
On screen Two "written hundreds of years before → fulfilled" rows, each with the prophecy quoted in full: Micah 5:2 ("And you, O Bethlehem… from you shall come out the ruler who will be the shepherd of my people") → Born in Bethlehem; Isaiah 7:14 ("a virgin shall conceive and bear a son") → Born of a virgin
"Two prophecies. That's all I'll give you. But here's the thing: these were written hundreds of years before Jesus was born. The odds of one person accidentally fulfilling both? Astronomically low. This is God's fingerprint."

Notes: Resist the urge to add more. Two lands harder than six. If someone asks for a third, tell them to go read Matthew's birth narrative — it's a better answer than you are.

21

How to read the Bible (YouCAt #16)

7 min
On screen Read it prayerfully, with the Holy Spirit · read the whole message, not isolated verses · interpret it with Jesus Christ at the center · trust the Magisterium (Pope & Bishops) for authentic interpretation — beside the CCC 137 quote, shown in full
"Interpretation of the inspired Scripture must be attentive above all to what God wants to reveal through the sacred authors for our salvation." — CCC 137
"You don't need a theology degree to read the Bible. You need the Holy Spirit and a willingness to listen. It's not about picking out random verses — it's about reading the whole love letter."

Notes: Point 4 (the Magisterium) sometimes gets pushback from teens who think "the Bible alone." Don't argue theology — just say the Church has been reading this with the Holy Spirit for 2,000 years and never needed a new rulebook, then move on. The CCC 137 quote is the authority for the whole slide.

22

Reflection questions

8 min
On screen Three questions in a notes-app frame, revealed one at a time
  • Before today, what did you think the Bible was? Has that changed at all?
  • If it's God's love letter to you, what's one small realistic step you could take this week? One verse a day. No pressure.
  • Which of Jesus' teachings is hardest for you to actually live out? Why?

Notes: Give them real time. Journal for 4 minutes in silence, then small groups for 4. Reveal one question at a time so the screen isn't a wall of text.

23

Closing prayer — "Come, Holy Spirit"

3 min
On screen The first eight lines of "Come, Holy Spirit, live in us", split into two left-aligned columns. Full text on screen at once, nothing to click through.
"Come, Holy Spirit, live in us, with God the Father and the Son, and grant us your abundant grace to sanctify and make us one. May mind and tongue made strong in love your praise throughout the world proclaim, and may that love within our hearts set fire to others with its flame."

Notes: Read it out loud rather than singing it, unless the group knows it well enough to lead it. Lights down if you can. These two slides replace the old "Let us pray" cues, the Our Father, and the dismissal slides.

Closing prayer — doxology

2 min
On screen "Most blessèd Trinity of love, / For whom the heart of man was made, / To you be praise in timeless song, / And everlasting homage paid." — single left-aligned column, full text shown.
