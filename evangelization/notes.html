1

Evangelization — L8, St Joseph B.T.Open slide ↗

Settle the room, then frame it in one sentence. The point of today is not how to win an argument for the faith — it is what we do with what we have already been given.

Don't linger here. This slide is a door, not a destination.

2–3

Opening prayer — "Heavenly Father, pour forth your Holy Spirit…"Open slide ↗

Said, not explained. Pause before you start and a beat after the amen. No commentary on the theology — it all comes back later in the talk.

If it is your custom for the class to stand, invite them to before you begin rather than after.

Note the last petition — hear the call of the evangelisation. That is the assignment for the rest of the session.

4

If your friends ask what you actually do at church on Sundays, what would you say?Open slide ↗

First real question of the talk — actually put it to the room and wait. Take three or four answers out loud.

Expect "pray, listen to the homily, Holy Mass." Let their words, not yours, become the next slide.

Do not correct or improve anyone's answer. Slide 5 hands their own words straight back to them.
5

Pray · Homily · EucharistOpen slide ↗

Just their answers, returned. Let it sit for two seconds before moving on.

Optional aside: that is the shape of a Sunday, not the whole of it. Which is exactly why the next question matters.

6

… then?Open slide ↗

The hinge of the whole talk. Pause and let the ellipsis do its work.

"…and then we're sent out. The Mass isn't the finish line — it's the send-off."

Then leave the question hanging. The answer is the next slide.

7

ITE, MISSA ESTOpen slide ↗

Every Mass ends with these words. Ite — go. Missa est — it is the sending.

Worth saying: the word "mission" and the word "Mass" share a root. Mass and mission are the same thing, and evangelisation is not an extra activity bolted onto the liturgy. It is what the Mass is for.

Also worth saying: the sending is God's before it is ours. We do not authorise the mission; we are sent in one.

The Mass ending — three forms

  1. Go forth, the Mass is ended The literal rendering of the Latin on the slide. Missa is the late-Latin form of missio, so it means the sending is made.
  2. Go and announce the Gospel of the Lord Ite ad Evangelium Domini nuntiandum. One of the two essential calls inside the sending: the outward one, carrying the Gospel to others.
  3. Go in peace, glorifying the Lord by your life Ite in pace, glorificando vita vestra Dominum. The other: the outward call is made good by the way you actually live.
The first translates the Latin; the other two unpack what that sending actually asks for. Both alternatives were approved for use in the Roman Rite, so if one of them comes at Mass, nothing unusual has happened — and both are worth listening for.
8

Isn't faith a private thing between me and God?Open slide ↗

Take a show of hands if you want. Most of the room will agree, and that's useful — it lets you say the Church agrees partly.

Faith is genuinely personal and intimate. Nobody is bluffing that. But the next slide shows where it stops being private.

Don't argue anyone out of their answer. Concede it, then turn.
9

God is Love, Truth, Beauty — Truth believed compels us to shareOpen slide ↗

Three ways we recognise God — and the first one is the engine for everything after this.

Truth believed compels us to share. This is the deck's own line, not a quotation — so don't attribute it to anyone if challenged. The solid support behind it is 1 Corinthians 9:16: Woe to me if I should preach not the gospel. Paul calls it a necessity laid on him, not a talent he happens to have. If truth is genuinely held, it cannot be held quietly.

"What have you believed that you have never told anybody?"

Don't collect answers. Let the question work on them.

10

Romans 10:14–17Open slide ↗

Walk the chain in verse 14 quickly — call, believe, hear, preacher, sent. Every single link requires somebody else to be involved.

That is the whole argument of the verse: you cannot complete it alone. Even the call presupposes something heard first.

Land on verse 17 — faith comes from what is heard, and hearing implies a voice. Someone spoke.

This is the answer to slide 8. Faith is not private, because it was never received privately.

11

Do I need to be an expert in arguments to evangelize?Open slide ↗

Second real question — put it to the room. The relief in the faces is the point. Almost nobody here feels equipped, and the next slide tells them they don't need to be.

12

Not apologetics — Plant seeds of love, not debatesOpen slide ↗

Draw the distinction cleanly: apologetics defends the faith; evangelisation shares a Person. They are different jobs.

Say the nuance out loud, or the slide will misfire. Apologetics is not strictly necessary — nobody is disqualified from sharing their faith because they cannot explain the Trinity or answer a question about suffering. It is helpful. When someone pushes back, having a reason ready keeps the conversation open instead of turning it into a fight.

So: not a prerequisite, genuinely useful. Both halves are true, and the second half is what stops "not apologetics" being heard as "apologetics is pointless".

The goal is not a debate won but a life seen. Keep the words "seeds" — you will call them back on slide 15.

13

My (or … Our?) grandma — No arguments, only faithful prayersOpen slide ↗

Tell a real story, briefly. Name her. Sixty seconds, concrete details — something she actually said or did. Specific beats impressive every time.

The point: nobody argued her into the faith. She prayed, and her children grew up watching that.

Then ask the room to remember theirs. One line, hands up, no commentary.
14

You scatter the seed — God gives the growthOpen slide ↗

The division of labour: we sow, God grows. Say it plainly — there is no pressure on you about the results.

15

Mark 4:26–29Open slide ↗

Slow down on verse 27 — he knows not how. That is deliberate. God does not explain the mechanism to the man scattering seed.

Point out the farmer's actual job in verse 26: scatter, sleep, rise night and day. It is almost entirely waiting.

This is slide 12 again — seeds, not speeches. The parable is about patience, not persuasion.

16

Are we trying to force or pressure people into believing?Open slide ↗

Take a beat. This is the question behind every aggressive comment-section evangelist, and it deserves a real answer rather than a deflection.

Concede the worry: some evangelising online genuinely is aggressive, and they have seen it.

17

Invitation, never coercionOpen slide ↗

The Church spreads the kingdom by persuasion, not coercion — the principle runs all the way back to Constantine and the early councils.

An invitation is something you extend, not something you enforce.

Practical version for them: if someone isn't ready, pray for them. Don't corner them. Especially online.
18

Mother Teresa — Served the dying and poor; their freedom fully respectedOpen slide ↗

Calcutta, Missionaries of Charity. Canonised 2016.

The detail to stress is the one in the subtitle — her freedom was fully respected. She never once forced a convert. She served first, and let the rest follow on its own.

She resisted the language of "charity" — she regarded it as justice, a matter of dignity rather than pity. Worth saying that people were treated as people, not as a strategy.

19

What if I don't have an epic conversion story of my own?Open slide ↗

This is the fear underneath most of the hesitation in the room: my faith isn't dramatic enough to be worth mentioning.

20

No personal epic needed — Share what inspires you in God's work in othersOpen slide ↗

You do not need a testimony. You need an observation.

What do you admire about someone else's faith? That is already enough to say out loud, and the next two slides prove it.

21

miracolieucaristici.orgOpen slide ↗

Pull the site up live if you have connectivity, otherwise describe it. Either way, let it be a surprise — the reveal works better cold.

A teenager built this. The exhibition is in Milan.

If you do go live, have the tab ready and know what the first screen shows. Don't fumble in front of the class.
22

Carlo Acutis — Retold Eucharistic miracles online; his passion spokeOpen slide ↗

Beatified 2020. Died in 2006, aged fifteen.

He asked to be buried in the field at Zelarino, outside Milan, so that people could come and pray there rather than come to look at him.

The point is not the miracles — it is that his love for the Eucharist was contagious online. No arguments, no apologetics. Just what he loved, and people caught it. Exactly slide 12.

23

Does my faith story have to be happy and blissful?Open slide ↗

Honest question, and probably true of someone in the room. Give it real air.

The pressure to present faith as constantly joyful is itself a kind of coercion — so say plainly that struggle is normal.

24

Real faith limps — Struggle belongs to the journeyOpen slide ↗

Not a slogan, a fact. In the Bible, faith is very often a wrestling match rather than a smooth ride.

Don't romanticise suffering, and don't let anyone disclose something heavy in front of the whole room. Keep it general and move on.
25

Jacob — Wrestled with God, walked away with a limpOpen slide ↗

Genesis 32. Worth noting who he is before he wrestles: a fugitive who has just talked his way out of a confrontation with Esau by pretending to be Esau.

He does not go into the night strong. He comes out limping.

26

Genesis 32:24–31Open slide ↗

Slow down here. This is one of the landing points.

The heart of it is verse 26: he holds on and will not let go until he is blessed. I will not let you go, unless you bless me. Even a schemer ends up asking God for a blessing, because he cannot manufacture one himself.

He walks away with a new name — Israel — and a limp. Both at once. That is how it works; you are not choosing between them.

Do not rush the last line. Limping because of his thigh. Pause after it.

If asked about the name change: the text says he wrestled with God and with men and prevailed. Israel is the one who has struggled with God.

27

Evangelii Gaudium 150Open slide ↗

Pope Francis, 2013. People prefer witnesses to experts.

They thirst for authenticity — they can tell the difference between someone performing a faith and someone living one.

"Your grandmother was not authentic because she was polished. She was authentic because she was real."

This is the payoff for slide 13. Authenticity is not polish. It is recognisable because it is not designed.

28

Will I get to see the results of sharing my faith?Open slide ↗

The real fear behind the fear: will any of it matter?

Honest answer: almost certainly not in the way you expect or get to watch.

29

The ripple outlives you — Results reach far beyond your lifetimeOpen slide ↗

Stone in water. You are always looking at the ripple, never at the stone. The ripple is the part that lasts.

The next three slides are the evidence.

30

St. Francis Xavier — A legacy that continues centuries laterOpen slide ↗

"Apostle to the Indies." Japan, India, and parts of Southeast Asia. Died on Shangchuan Island in 1552.

One man, four and a half centuries ago, and the ripple is still moving. That is the entire argument of this slide.

31

Where St. Francis Xavier is honoredOpen slide ↗

Name the actual place out loud. This is the slide that turns four centuries into their own city, and it only works if you say where it is.

Fill this in before you present — the image caption is generic on purpose.

Point out that people still go there. If anyone in the class has been, let them say so.

32

Statue of St. Francis XavierOpen slide ↗

Let the image sit. Don't talk over it.

If the mood allows, make it practical: there is a place you can go and stand in front of him. Maybe suggest someone does.

33

Would you share love with an enemy…?Open slide ↗

The hardest slide in the deck. Give it room and do not rush past it.

Do not ask for hands here. Nobody wants to answer that in public at fifteen, and putting them on the spot is exactly the coercion of slide 17.

Just let the question sit, then go to the answer.

34

Strongest when you least feel itOpen slide ↗

The honest paradox: you share best when you don't feel like it.

Because at your worst it stops being about you and becomes about them. Your feelings are not the qualification.

35

St. John Paul II — Visited his assassin in prison, and forgave himOpen slide ↗

The answer to slide 33. Slow down.

Two months after being shot in St Peter's Square, he went to the prison and forgave the man who did it. Say the interval — two months — because that is what makes it remarkable rather than easy.

36

St. Thérèse, age 14 — Prayed for a criminal's conversionOpen slide ↗

Thérèse of Lisieux. She was fourteen. Lead with that — it lands immediately with this audience.

She prayed for a man condemned to death, and according to her account he converted before his execution. If anyone presses on the details: her account mentions the event, though exactly which prisoner was involved is disputed. Don't get drawn into it.

She had already decided her life was for other people. So can they.

37

Sharing Your Story in 3 StepsOpen slide ↗

The practical turn. Everything before this was preparation for it.

Pre-empt the main objection: this isn't preaching. It is answering three questions about your own life. Nobody is being asked to make a speech or to convince anybody of anything.

If time allows, model one of the three in your own words. Not a polished version — a real one. Authenticity, slide 27.
38–39

Closing prayer — "Most Holy Virgin Mary…"Open slide ↗

Let it be prayed. Slow the pace right down and do not rush the amen.

The second petition consecrates the New Evangelisation for Singapore. That ties straight back to slide 7 — the sending, made specific, for this place and these people.

Close by leaving it there. No summary speech; the prayer is the summary.
